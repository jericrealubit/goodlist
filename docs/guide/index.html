In every picture, the red dotted box shows you exactly where to tap, and the red number tells you the order. That is the whole trick — find the red box, tap it.

The four buttons at the bottom. They never change. Tap one and the page changes — that is all they do, and you cannot break anything by tapping them.

  • Tasks — your list of things to do. This is the main page.
  • Group — the people you share with. Empty at first, and that is fine.
  • History — everything you have already finished.
  • Settings — your name, the colours, and signing out.

Part 1 — Just you

1.Make your account

You do not need anybody else to use Goodlist. It works perfectly well on your own.

Open Goodlist. The first thing it asks is who you are.

  • Type your name in the first box. You can skip this one.
  • Type your email in the second box.
  • Make up a password and type it twice, so the app knows you did not mistype it. It has to be 6 letters or numbers or more.
  • Tap Create account.
If it says Check your email — go to your email, open the message from Goodlist, and tap the link inside. Then come back to the app.

2.Log in

Already made an account? Type your email and password, then tap Log in. You only have to do this once — the app remembers you afterwards.

Forgot your password? Tap Forgot password? just under the button. Goodlist emails you a link to make a new one.

3.Add your first thing to do

The page says Solo mode at the top. Solo just means on your own. The list is empty because you have not put anything in it yet.

At the bottom there is a long white box that says I want to...

  • Tap that box. The keyboard comes up.
  • Type the thing you want to do. Anything at all.
  • Tap the round green arrow next to the box.

That is it. The box empties itself so you can type the next one straight away. Add as many as you like.

4.Look at your list

Every task sits on its own little white card.

  • The empty circle on the left means not done yet.
  • Small grey writing underneath tells you extra things, like a due date.
  • A task with a line through it is one you already finished.
To move a task up or down: press your finger on it, hold, and slide it where you want. Let go. Put the important ones at the top.

5.Tick something off

There are two ways to finish a task, and both do exactly the same thing:

  • Tap the circle on the left of the task, or
  • slide the task to the right with your finger. A green ✓ Done appears behind it.
Ticked the wrong one? Tap the circle again. It comes straight back.

6.Change a task

Tap the words of a task — not the circle — to open it. Here you can add a note, add a due date, and tap Save changes.

Mark complete is the same as ticking the circle. Delete task throws it away completely.

Deleting cannot be undone — that is why the button is red. To leave without changing anything, tap the arrow at the top left.

7.Find things you finished

Tap History at the bottom. Everything you have finished lives here, newest first, with the day and time you did it.

  • ↺ puts a task back on your to-do list.
  • 🗑 removes it forever.
Delete all empties the whole history in one go. It asks you first, because this cannot be undone.

Part 2 — You and other people

8.Open the Group page

Everything above still works the same. This part only adds to it. A group is a small set of people — your family, or a small team — who can ask each other to do things.

Your own list stays private. Nobody else in the group can ever see it. The only things anybody else sees are the ones you specifically ask them to do.

Tap Group at the bottom. You only need one of the two buttons:

  • Create a group — you are the first one. Pick this if you are setting it up.
  • Join a group — somebody already made one and gave you a code.

9.Make your group

  • Give it a name. Anything you will recognise.
  • Family or Team? Pick one. It only changes the words in the next list.
  • Your role. A Family offers Father, Mother, Guardian, Child, Other. A Team offers Leader and Member.
  • Tap Create group.

The group exists, and right now you are the only one in it.

10.Give out your code

Your group now has a code — eight letters and numbers. This is how somebody joins, and nobody can get in without it.

Tap Share to send it by message or email. Reading it out loud works just as well.

A code never uses a zero, a letter O, a one, or a letter I. That is on purpose, so nobody mixes them up.

11.They type in the code

The next two steps happen on the other person’s phone, not yours.

They install Goodlist, make their own account, then tap Group, then Join a group. They type the code you gave them and tap Continue.

12.They pick who they are

The app shows them the group name so they know it is the right one. They pick their own role and tap Join group.

You are now in a group together, and both of you will see the other person’s name on the Group page.

13.Your list now has two parts

Go back to Tasks. There are two words at the top now.

  • Personal — your own list, exactly as before. Still private, still only yours.
  • Requested — things you and your group have asked each other to do.

The name at the very top changed too: instead of Solo mode it now shows your group’s name.

14.Ask someone to do something

Tap Requested, type what you would like them to do, and tap the green arrow.

Tap it to add a note or a due date, just like before. Changed your mind? Open it and tap Cancel request.

More than one other person in your group? A row of names appears above the typing box. Tap a name first, then type — that is how the app knows who you mean.

15.What they see

On the other person’s phone, the same task looks like this:

  • A red number appears on the Tasks button — how many new requests they have. It disappears once they look.
  • The task says From and your name, so they know who asked.
  • They tick the circle when it is done.

The moment they tick it, it leaves your Requested list and lands in History for both of you. You do not have to refresh anything.

Nice to know

None of this is essential. It is here when you want it.

  • Display name is what other people in your group see. Change it in Settings and tap Save.
  • Theme changes all the colours — there are nine. Nothing else changes, so pick whichever you like looking at.
  • The privacy switch decides whether your country counts towards the “where is Goodlist used” numbers. It uses your phone’s time zone, never your actual location.
  • No internet? Keep going. Add tasks and tick them off as normal. A small message says you are offline, and everything saves once your signal is back.
  • Two groups, no more. A family group and a work team, but that is the limit.
  • Who can delete what. Only the person who made a task can delete it. If somebody asked you to do something you can finish it or un-finish it, but not throw it away — it is theirs.
  • Who is in charge of a group. Whoever made it. That person can rename it, remove somebody, or hand the job over with Make owner. Everybody else can simply leave.

If something goes wrong

It says the invite code is invalid. Check every character and type it again slowly. There is never a zero or a letter O in a code. If it still will not work, ask for a fresh copy.

You tapped a circle by accident. Tap it again and it comes back. If you already left the page, go to History and tap ↺.

You cannot see a friend’s tasks. That is correct — you are not meant to. You only ever see the things somebody has specifically asked you to do.

The Requested tab is missing. It only appears once you are in a group. Go to Group and create or join one.

You cannot delete your account. If you own a group that still has other people in it, Goodlist stops you — otherwise you would take their group away. Hand it over with Make owner, or remove the other members first.

Almost nothing in Goodlist is permanent. Anything you tick can be un-ticked, and anything you delete asks you first.
